A few to start with

A few quick tips, straight from the full articles above.

Reconcile monthly, not yearly

A discrepancy caught 30 days late is far easier to trace than one found 11 months later at tax time.

Separate business and personal spending

Commingled accounts are one of the most common — and most expensive — bookkeeping cleanups we see.

Set aside taxes as you're paid

Pulling a percentage from every payment as it arrives beats scrambling to find it in April.

Track expenses in real time

Reconstructing a year of receipts and mileage from memory rarely holds up as well as logging them as they happen.

Use classes or tags in QuickBooks

Splitting revenue by service line or location turns one big number into something you can actually act on.

Reconcile your bank feed weekly

Letting transactions pile up in the "for review" queue is exactly where miscategorization creeps in.

Review your P&L monthly

Trends are easier to catch — and fix — early than after a full year has already gone by.

Profit isn't the same as cash

A business can be profitable on paper and still run short on cash if payment timing doesn't line up.

Specialize if you're building a bookkeeping practice

Focusing on one or two industries makes marketing easier and often supports higher rates than staying a generalist.

Learn to explain the numbers, not just enter them

Clients pay more for a bookkeeper who can interpret a report than one who only does data entry.

Never ignore an IRS notice

Most notices carry a response deadline — missing it can narrow your options even when the underlying issue is minor.

Check the notice type before you panic

The code in the corner (like CP2000 or CP14) tells you what kind of notice it is — some are routine, not urgent.
